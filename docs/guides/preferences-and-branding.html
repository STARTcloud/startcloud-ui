STARTcloud Universal Branding

The picture of the Preferences, Language & Branding Contract on the authorization server's four sites; where the two disagree, the contract's text wins. Today the issuer paints its auth pages from auth.css, one [data-theme] block per site composing surfaces, accent and display face in one attribute. On the shared chrome the same four sites are two independent axes: the variant, light or dark, chosen by the person and stamped as data-bs-theme, and the pack, a bare brand name stamped as data-brand, whose stylesheet carries colours, surfaces, artwork and the auth column's face, never geometry. The site's own default variant travels as brand.theme in /api/status, answered per site by hostname, and the pack and its stylesheet come from the branding endpoint. The first frame is live: pick a site and a variant, and the chrome, the sign-in column and the footer repaint from the pack's variables alone.

a yellow note marks what this design changes against the sites as auth.css paints them today
Live — /login on each site, the variant the person's, the pack the site's; the pre-paint script applies brand.theme until the person chooses
What this frame proves. One chrome, one sign-in column, four looks, and every difference is a value of a pack file: --brand-primary, --brand-on-primary, the mark, the surfaces where the pack names them and, for the auth column alone, the display face. Moonshine's greys and Nomad's purple-black are pack values under the dark variant; a person on the Moonshine.dev site who picks light gets Moonshine's green on whatever light surfaces the pack names, and Bootstrap's where it names none.
Changed: two attributes, never composed. <html data-theme="moonshinedev"> becomes <html data-bs-theme="dark" data-brand="moonshinedev">; <html data-theme="light"> becomes data-bs-theme="light" with no data-brand at all, because a site on theme_id: light publishes theme_pack: null.
Changed: surfaces are the pack's to set. The surface palettes of auth.css move into the packs as --bs-* colours per variant: Moonshine's neutral greys and Nomad's purple-black under [data-brand][data-bs-theme="dark"], a light twin where the site wants one; the stock variant is the fallback wherever a pack names nothing, and geometry is never a pack's.
Changed: the person chooses. The auth pages never had a theme button; the chrome's cluster has one on every page, the choice writes through PATCH /api/user/preferences, and the site's theme_id only seeds brand.theme for the first paint of a visitor with no choice.
The four sites at rest — each on its own default variant, the pack's accent, mark and display face
STARTcloud is theme_id: light, so no pack: stock Bootstrap, the chrome's own primary, the STARTcloud mark from brand.logoUrl, Source Serif 4 for the auth headline because that is the shared auth column's face.
Moonshine.dev is a dark-default site with a green accent (#1f9d57) and the crescent-M mark; its auth.css block says the headline takes the body sans, so the pack names IBM Plex Sans as its display face.
SwitchBoard.cloud is light with the cyan (#24ADE3, the design system's --brand-primary) and Poppins headings, the one site whose block overrides accent and face only and leaves the surfaces to the variant already; it draws no footer.
Nomad Services is a dark-default site with the purple (#6c5ce7) and the gear-N mark, headings in the body sans.
Changed: the display face. The branding contract keeps typography out of v1; decision 5 of the identity contract lets a pack name the auth column's display face and nothing else. That is the one font a pack carries, the chrome stays on Montserrat and Open Sans, and a pack naming no face gets Source Serif 4.

Changed: the branding endpoint's shape. Today SiteBrandingController answers site_id, theme_pack, company_name and three logo strings; the contract's shape is the one above: theme_css returned, absolute URLs, every logo slot an object with width and height, theme_pack: null for a light site; and the path moves from /public/site/branding to /api/public/site/branding, because programs call /api and /public/* is the SPA's.
Changed: the footer flags. sites.switchboard.ui.show_footer_on_* are six per-page booleans; the shared chrome draws the footer per UI backend through the footer token, so one switch per site, sites.<id>.ui.footer, decides whether that site's status lists the word, every page of the site alike, and the six flags go. A white-label site drops its footer and its "Powered by" line with one value, and one issuer serves branded and white-label sites from the same build.
Utility links. links.docs is sites.<id>.assets.help_url and links.contact is mailto: the site's mail.support_email; none of the four sites sets either today, so the signed-out top bar carries the brand, the theme button and the flag alone.
The pack's variable names are the contract's: --brand-primary, --brand-on-primary, the optional --brand-logo and --brand-logo-color, plus the --bs-* bridge; --brand-auth-display is the one addition decision 5 makes; the generator computes --brand-on-primary as white or #212529, whichever passes 4.5:1 against the accent, when the YAML omits it, so Moonshine.dev and SwitchBoard.cloud read dark on their accents and Nomad Services white, and refuses a pack where neither passes.

brand.theme is the site's own value. sites.<id>.ui.default_theme, light or dark, beside the site's other ui switches; today light and switchboard paint light surfaces in auth.css and moonshinedev and nomadservices dark ones, which is what the four sites' values start as, and theme_id only names the pack from then on.
The pack registry is the shared UI's: one directory per pack under public/themes/<pack>/ beside public/brand/, a package of files a developer writes, the stylesheet, the artwork and the font files, shipped with the build and served by the origin that serves it; the authorization server holds no pack and renders none, it names one per site through theme_id and answers theme_css as that stylesheet's URL with its content hash as ?v= and a long max-age, the URL built from the site's configured hostname and never the request's Host; the branding answer itself is public, max-age=300. On the issuer a pack is same-origin unless the site lists the host in sites.<id>.ui.pack_origins, each listed origin becoming that site's style-src and font-src entry, because a stylesheet from a host the issuer does not control runs on the sign-in page (RFC 9700 §4.2.4). The site marks live under public/brand/<site>/ in the same build, since the jar serves no /assets.
Per-instance SwitchBoard keeps the central site's pack and marks; only the hostname and the registration flags differ, as application-instance.yml already does.
